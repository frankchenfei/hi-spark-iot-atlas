IoT certification requirements in United States

Radio approval, safety listing, children's data and state privacy rules all apply to connected products sold in the United States.

10 certification records apply · last reviewed 2026-10-14

FCC

U.S. equipment authorization rules for radio transmitters, intentional radiators and certain electronic products placed on the American market.

What to prepare

  • Identify the correct authorization route for each radio
  • Test to the applicable FCC rule part at an accredited lab
  • Keep the FCC ID label and user manual statements accurate
  • Complete the supplier’s declaration or certification filing
  • List the module grant conditions when using a pre-certified module
  • Identify the correct UL or CSA standard for the product
  • Submit samples and critical components to the NRTL
  • Accept the factory inspection programme for listed products

Frequently asked questions

Which certifications are mandatory to sell an IoT device in United States?

FCC (FCC equipment authorization), UL / ETL (North American safety listing), Prop 65 (California Proposition 65), CCPA / CPRA (California consumer privacy rights), COPPA (Children’s online privacy (COPPA)). Each record lists the issuing body, what it covers and what it does not cover.

Are voluntary marks needed as well?

Energy Star - Energy Star efficiency programme; NIST 8259 - NIST IR 8259 IoT cybersecurity baseline; SOC 2 - SOC 2 trust services report; US Cyber Trust Mark - US Cyber Trust Mark

Where can I read the official requirement?

Every certification record links to the issuing body or the regulation text. Start from this page, open the record and follow its source links.

Does a certificate for a module cover the finished product?

No. A module certificate covers the module; the finished device usually needs its own approval or a documented module grant reference. Confirm with the issuing body or your test laboratory.
